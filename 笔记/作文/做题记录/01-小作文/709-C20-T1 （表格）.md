The first table provides information about changes in the total population of New York City between 1800 and 2000. The second and third tables show population changes across the city’s five districts over the same period.

Overall, the total population of New York City rose dramatically over the 200-year period. At the same time, Manhattan’s share of the total population fell consistently. By contrast, the proportion of people living in the other four districts increased significantly over this 200-year period.

To begin with, New York City had a population of 79,216 in 1800, and this figure rose continuously to reach 8,009,185 in 2000. Manhattan’s population stood at 60,515 in 1800. It peaked at 1,850,093 in 1900 and then declined to 1,538,096 in 2000. Meanwhile, its share of the city’s total population dropped from 76% to 19%.

In contrast, both the population and the percentage share of the other four districts (Brooklyn, Bronx, Queens and Staten Island) increased over this period. Their total population saw a steady increase from 18,701 to 6,471,089, and its proportion of the city’s total rose from 24% to 81%.
